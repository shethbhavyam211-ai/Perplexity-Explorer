How It Works

A beginner-friendly breakdown of language model uncertainty, two equivalent formulas, and score interpretation.

What is Perplexity?

In plain terms, perplexity measures how surprised the model is by what it reads. If a model predicts the exact next word with 100% certainty, its surprise is zero and its perplexity is 1. If it is completely baffled and has to guess uniformly among 100 possible words, its perplexity is 100. Lower perplexity always means the model is more confident and natural.

Logarithmic Formulation (Standard in AI)
PPL = exp( − (1/N) × Σ ln P(tokeni) )
N = Sequence Length P(tokeni) = Conditional Likelihood Σ = Sum Over All Tokens exp = Natural Exponential
Simple Formula (Direct Multiplication)
PPL = ( 1 / ( P1 × P2 × … × PN ) )(1/N)
N = Number of Tokens P₁ × P₂ × … × Pₙ = Joint Sequence Probability 1 / Product = Inverted Surprise (1/N) Power = N-th Geometric Root

How to Read the Result

A low perplexity means the model found the text predictable and unsurprising. A high perplexity means the text was erratic, surprising, or completely outside its training distribution.
